Design decisions log
====================

Informal record of why things are built the way they are. Newest first.


2026-07 — Urgency vocabulary drift
----------------------------------
Acknowledged rather than fixed: the triage package talks about "severity"
and "priority", the database column is "urgency", and the notification
service asks whether an item "requires immediate attention". These grew up
at different times (triage was prototyped in a separate repo). Renaming
everything to one word would churn every module for zero behavior change,
so we documented the mapping in architecture.md and moved on.


2026-06 — Word-boundary keyword matching
----------------------------------------
The original substring matcher flagged a five-star review containing
"beautifully fired ceramic" as a fire hazard. Matching now requires word
boundaries: "fire" matches "fire" and "caught fire" but not "fired".
Phrases in the keyword lists are matched as whole phrases. The trade-off
(missing genuine safety reports phrased in words we didn't list) is
handled by keeping the term lists generous rather than by loosening the
matcher.


2026-05 — Billing history feeds triage
--------------------------------------
A single "payment failed" message reads as routine, but the third one in a
month from the same customer is a pattern. The repeated-billing-failure
rule therefore consults the database (count of the customer's recent
billing complaints) in addition to the message text. This is the only rule
that looks beyond the submission itself; keep it that way unless there is
a strong reason — rules that hit the database are harder to reason about
and test.


2026-04 — Escalation is a separate decision from urgency
--------------------------------------------------------
Urgency answers "how serious is this?"; escalation answers "does the
on-duty team need to see it now?". Conflating them meant every high item
paged the team, including shipping complaints nobody could act on because
the customer gave no order reference. The escalation policy owns per-
category conditions; triage stays category-generic where possible.


2026-04 — Notification outbox instead of direct send
----------------------------------------------------
We do not send email/Slack from the request path. The notification service
writes rows to a notifications table (an outbox) and a delivery process
owns actually sending them. Benefits: the intake path cannot be slowed or
failed by a provider outage, suppressed alerts leave an auditable record,
and tests don't need network mocks. Consequence to remember: "a
notification exists" and "the team was alerted" are related but distinct
facts — check the row's status.


2026-04 — Baseline severities are fallbacks, not competitors
------------------------------------------------------------
First implementation let the per-category baseline compete with specific
rules under a max-wins policy, which meant a compliment (low) could never
beat the product baseline (normal). Baselines are now consulted only when
no specific rule fires.
